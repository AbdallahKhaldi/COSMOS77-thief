// READ THIS FIRST — 2 MINUTES

The big picture

The game: our team (cosmos77) has two programs — a COP and a THIEF. A match against another team is a series of 6 sub-games on a 7×7 city grid. In each sub-game our program plays against one of theirs (always cop-vs-thief, never cop-vs-cop), they talk to each other over the internet, and every move is cryptographically sealed so nobody can cheat. At the end, both teams' programs check each other's math and must agree on the result.

"Their engine or ours?" — both, always. There is no host: our two programs run our code, their two programs run theirs, and they only exchange messages. The only thing YOU choose is where our programs run: on our cloud server (default — you run nothing) or on your laptop (only if the other team insists on dialing an IP address with ports 8801/8802).

Who plays which sub-game? Automatic rule the whole class uses: sort the two team names alphabetically — the FIRST one plays cop in sub-games 1,3,5, the second in 2,4,6. Both of our current opponents sort before "cosmos77", so they are cop on 1,3,5 and we are cop on 2,4,6. The command generator below computes this for you — never guess it.

Three kinds of games: F1 friendly one sub-game, a smoke test, zero risk · F2 friendly the full 6, a dress rehearsal, zero risk · COUNTED the one that decides the grade — exactly ONE counted game per opponent counts (rule 52), so it only runs after a clean F2.

Why we do it this way (30 seconds)

The course grade is league placement: you must play counted games against at least two different teams, and every counted game must end with BOTH teams filing reports that agree. If a report is missing or the two disagree, both teams get zero — so the whole procedure below exists to make the counted game boring and provable: practice first (friendlies cost nothing), compare one fingerprint to prove both sides recorded the same result, then play the one that counts and file the same day.

Everyone in the class builds against the same community kit, so all teams speak the same protocol and expect the same fields. That is why this page is plug-and-play: the steps do not change from opponent to opponent — only their group id and their two addresses.
